Section OneContext

97% on Roads — and That Is the Problem

Ghana moves by truck. Almost exclusively by truck. A road network under maintenance pressure, fragmented operators, no cold chain at scale, and a World Bank logistics score of 2.4 out of 5. The gap is structural — and the opportunity it creates is commercial.

Ghana's transportation system is functional but fragile. 97% of all freight and passenger movement runs by road — a near-total dependence on a single mode that creates systemic vulnerability every time a key corridor floods, a bridge fails, or fuel prices spike. Rail contributes less than 1%. The port handles 26.5 million tonnes of cargo annually and growing, but the logistics chain connecting it to production zones and end consumers operates largely through informal arrangements, fragmented ownership, and analogue systems.

The World Bank's 2022 Logistics Performance Index placed Ghana at 2.4 out of 5 — below Kenya at 3.0, Morocco at 3.2, and the 3.2 threshold that signals genuine regional hub capability. The sub-scores are precise: infrastructure quality, customs efficiency, and tracking capability are the three weakest dimensions. Each is commercially addressable. Ghana does not need new roads to become West Africa's leading logistics country — it needs the cold chain, digital platforms, trained workforce, and fleet management infrastructure that converts an adequate physical network into a reliable, high-performing logistics system.

Ghana is 80% of the way to being West Africa's logistics hub. It has the port, the geographic position, the road network, and the political stability. What it is missing is the cold chain, the platform layer, and the trained workforce that make logistics reliable enough for high-value goods and time-sensitive supply chains.

Section TwoCold Chain

$1.9 Billion Lost Every Year to a Solvable Problem

40–50% of Ghana's perishable agricultural output spoils before reaching a consumer. Not for lack of demand — for lack of the cold chain between farm and market. This is not a public goods problem. It is a commercial logistics gap with a $900 million annual revenue prize waiting on the other side.

Ghana produces tomatoes, mangoes, peppers, fish, and root crops at scale. What it does not produce is the unbroken refrigerated chain — from farm collection through aggregation, transport, and market — that allows those products to travel more than a few hours without spoilage. The result: $1.9 billion in annual post-harvest losses, concentrated in perishables. 50% of tomato output is lost. 45% of fruits. 35% of fish and seafood. These are not statistical abstractions — they represent farming households that planted, tended, and harvested crops that never generated income.

Cold Chain Ghana, BRIDGE's anchor transport venture, is built around one commercial insight: the $1.9 billion in spoilage is the addressable market. Solar-powered storage at agricultural aggregation points eliminates the grid dependency that caused previous cold storage programmes to fail. The Agricultural Collection Network provides reliable feedstock. And the economics are direct — each tonne of produce that arrives intact instead of spoiled is revenue the cold chain captures. $900 million per year in cold chain revenue potential sits on the other side of this infrastructure gap. BRIDGE builds the infrastructure that bridges it.

Section ThreeFragmentation

80% of Truckers Own Fewer Than 5 Trucks — and That Is the Core Constraint

The structural barrier to Ghana's logistics modernisation is not capital. It is fragmentation. Operators too small to invest in systems, training, or technology — but collectively large enough that BRIDGE's platforms can aggregate what fragmentation cannot.

Ghana's trucking industry is structurally unable to self-modernise. With 80% of operators owning fewer than 5 vehicles, no individual operator has the scale to invest in fleet management software, telematics, driver training programmes, or the maintenance infrastructure that reduces vehicle downtime. The economics are clear: a 3-truck owner cannot justify a $50,000 telematics investment. A fleet management platform serving 500 such operators can deliver the same functionality at $200 per truck per year — and profit doing it.

The same logic applies to empty backhauls. Individual small operators have no visibility into return-route cargo availability — they run empty because they lack the information infrastructure to fill the truck. 30–50% of all journeys run empty. BRIDGE's Load Matching Platform connects shippers with carriers across the full network, eliminating the backhaul rate that represents pure waste for operators and inflated transport costs for every business that ships goods in Ghana. The platform does not require operators to merge or grow — it makes their existing trucks more productive through information alone.

Trucking Industry Structure and Sector Efficiency Gaps
80% of Ghana's trucking operators own fewer than 5 trucks — below the threshold for technology investment. Only 12% of vehicles carry telematics, 22% of drivers hold formal training credentials, and 8% of operators use any digital system. Each gap is commercially addressable: Fleet Management and Load Matching platforms deliver modern logistics capability to fragmented operators without requiring consolidation.

Section FourLogistics Performance

2.4 Today. 3.2 by 2030. Kenya-Level Performance Is the Target

Ghana's LPI of 2.4 costs it export competitiveness, investor confidence, and the regional hub status its port capacity and geographic position should command. The gap is specific and closeable — BRIDGE's portfolio addresses every sub-dimension directly.

The World Bank Logistics Performance Index measures six dimensions: customs efficiency, infrastructure quality, international shipment ease, logistics competence, tracking capability, and delivery timeliness. Ghana's 2.4 reflects addressable deficits in each — not a fundamental infrastructure limitation. Infrastructure quality is constrained by cold chain absence and road maintenance gaps. Logistics competence is limited by the training shortage. Tracking capability is near-zero because the platforms that provide it don't yet exist at scale. Customs efficiency is improving but remains a friction point for cross-border trade under AfCFTA.

BRIDGE's portfolio addresses every LPI sub-dimension directly. The Logistics Training Academy builds competence at scale. Load Matching and Agricultural Collection platforms add tracking and visibility. Cold Chain Ghana adds critical infrastructure quality for temperature-sensitive goods — the single highest-weight gap. Cross-Border Facilitation reduces customs friction. A coordinated BRIDGE programme can move Ghana from 2.4 to Kenya-equivalent 3.0 within the first investment cycle, and to Morocco-comparable 3.2 — the regional hub threshold — by 2030.

Logistics Performance Index: Ghana vs. Regional & Global Benchmarks
Ghana's 2.4 LPI sits below the sub-Saharan peer average and significantly behind Morocco's 3.2 and South Africa's 3.4. The 0.8-point gap to BRIDGE's 2030 target is not infrastructure — it is the platform, training, and cold chain layer that converts adequate physical infrastructure into a high-performing logistics system. Tema Port Throughput & Ghana's Role as Regional Transit Hub
Tema Port handled 26.5 million tonnes and 1.35 million TEUs in 2023, with container traffic projected to reach 1.52M TEU by 2025. Critically, approximately 72% of transit traffic serves landlocked neighbours — Burkina Faso (38%), Mali (22%), Niger (12%). Ghana's logistics performance improvement is not only a domestic issue: better logistics attracts more landlocked trade through Tema, generating transit revenue and cementing Ghana's hub position across West Africa.

Logistics Is the Connective Tissue for Every Sector BRIDGE Invests In

There is no agricultural value chain without reliable transport. No manufacturing competitiveness without supply chain logistics. No health system quality without pharmaceutical cold chain. Transportation is not a standalone sector — it is the infrastructure layer that determines whether every other BRIDGE investment delivers at full potential.

Cross-Sector Transport Linkages

Agriculture & Value Chains: The Agricultural Collection Network and Cold Chain Ghana are the transport arm of BRIDGE's entire agriculture portfolio. Every cooperative investment, every post-harvest loss reduction, every export-grade produce target depends on getting produce to market cold and on time. These sectors are operationally inseparable.

Manufacturing & Light Industry: Supply chain logistics is a direct input cost for every manufacturer. Load Matching reduces transport costs for raw material inputs and finished goods distribution. Cross-Border Facilitation opens the AfCFTA export corridors that BRIDGE's manufacturing ventures depend on to reach the 1.4 billion consumer market.

Health Systems: Pharmaceutical Cold Chain directly addresses the 25% vaccine spoilage rate undermining Ghana's immunisation coverage. Last-Mile Delivery extends to medical supply distribution in rural health facilities. Cold chain infrastructure serves health outcomes as directly as it serves commercial logistics.

Financial Inclusion: Vehicle Asset Financing uses telematics data for alternative credit scoring — creating a new financial inclusion channel for informal operators who have no collateral but have operating history. Load Matching's mobile money integration drives financial account activity across the transport workforce.

Technology & Innovation: Load Matching and Fleet Management are software platforms — each one a technology venture as much as a logistics venture. The telematics network they build is Ghana's first real-time commercial freight tracking layer, with data that benefits every sector that ships goods.
