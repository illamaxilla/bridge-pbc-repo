① Model Markets — DACF 25% Mandate

The 25% DACF allocation requires all 260+ district assemblies to invest in Model Market construction. At GH₵2.2B+ total, this creates a mandatory, government-funded replication market for BRIDGE's Kejetia digitization platform. The playbook is built; the funding is legislated.

Entry Point

Position as technical partner and platform operator for the 260+ district Model Market programme. Submit proposals to MLGDRD in Q1 2026.

② Oil Palm Finance Window

The National Policy on Integrated Oil Palm Development establishes a US$500M finance window with 5-year moratorium and 70% project financing. BRIDGE's agricultural processing ventures are purpose-built for this instrument.

Entry Point

Apply to the Oil Palm Finance Window as co-financing partner and processing anchor tenant under Grow24. Application window open 2026.

③ Connect24 Cold Chain & Logistics

The GH₵4.3B road investment under Connect24 creates the arterial network that BRIDGE's cold chain ventures require. Every dollar invested in last-mile cold chain operates on a 20× foundation of public infrastructure.

Entry Point

Cold chain network deployment timed to Connect24 road completion. Co-design with Ghana Shippers' Authority and Ghana Ports & Harbours Authority.

④ 24H+ Authority — Direct Partnership

The newly established Authority is seeking private sector partners to co-design implementation frameworks across all seven pillars. This is a rare opportunity to be embedded in the national implementation structure rather than responding from outside.

Entry Point

Submit BRIDGE partnership proposal to the 24-Hour Economy Authority before the Bill passes Parliament. The early mover advantage is real and time-limited.

Section Four Capital Architecture

The Blended Finance Architecture

How BRIDGE structures capital alongside public and development finance to maximize impact and manage risk

The 2026 Budget explicitly commits to keeping major investments "largely off the sovereign balance sheet" through GIIF, DBG, and private capital partnerships. This language was written for exactly the structured co-investment that BRIDGE deploys.

BRIDGE's four-layer capital stack. Each layer serves a distinct function in the risk-return architecture.
P&P Notes — Diaspora Equity (20–30%)

Patient capital from Ghanaian diaspora investors. First-loss position absorbs early-stage risk and provides mandate flexibility that commercial capital cannot offer.

DFI Co-Investment — Concessional Debt (30–40%)

GIIF, DBG, and development finance institutions provide concessional debt and guarantees. The 2026 budget explicitly enables these structures through Fund24.

Blended Finance Instruments (20%)

Government credit guarantees and results-based financing mechanisms activated by 2026 budget programmes — particularly Fund24 and DBG recapitalization.

Commercial Capital (20–30%)

Standard commercial investors attracted by de-risked structures and government co-investment signals. Highest return expectations; lowest risk exposure.

Co-investing alongside government capital is not just a risk management strategy. When private capital flows into the same sectors as public capital, the probability of systemic improvement compounds — and the BRIDGE portfolio becomes greater than the sum of its parts.
